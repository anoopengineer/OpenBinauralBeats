40 Hz binaural beats for focus

40 Hz is the most popular binaural beat for concentration and deep work. Here's what it is, why it gets so much attention, and how to get the most out of it.

What "40 Hz" means

A 40 Hz binaural beat plays one tone in your left ear and a tone 40 Hz higher in your right, for example 200 Hz and 240 Hz. Your brain perceives a fast, gentle flutter at 40 beats per second. That puts it in the gamma band, the fastest of the common brainwave bands, which is associated with attention, concentration and processing information.

If binaural beats are new to you, start with what binaural beats are.

Why 40 Hz gets so much attention

Gamma activity around 40 Hz shows up in the brain when people concentrate, and it has been studied for decades. More recently, researchers at MIT have studied 40 Hz light and sound stimulation in mice and in early human trials, looking at brain health. Those studies used flickering lights and clicking sounds, not binaural beats, so they don't tell us much about headphones and a focus app. But they're a big part of why 40 Hz became popular.

Research on binaural beats themselves is mixed. A 2019 meta-analysis found small to moderate effects on attention and memory, with a lot of variation between studies. Treat 40 Hz as a tool worth trying, not a guaranteed boost.

How to use 40 Hz binaural beats

  1. Use headphones. Each ear has to hear its own tone, so speakers won't work.
  2. Keep it quiet. Set the volume just loud enough to hear. It should sit in the background, not demand your attention.
  3. Pair it with a focus block. Start a 25 to 90 minute session timer, pick one task, and let the sound become your "work mode" signal.
  4. Add a little noise if you like. A low level of pink or brown noise softens the tone and masks office or household sounds.
  5. Give it a few sessions. The flutter can feel odd at first. Most people stop noticing it after a few minutes.

Recommended settings

  • Carrier: 200 Hz. Lower carriers sound warmer and are easier to listen to for long sessions.
  • Beat: 40 Hz.
  • Volume: 20–30% to start.
  • Session: 25 minutes for a Pomodoro, or 60–90 minutes for deep work.

The 40 Hz Focus preset in OpenBinauralBeats uses exactly these settings. You can also type in your own carrier, for example 150 Hz for a deeper sound or 300 Hz for a brighter one.

40 Hz or 20 Hz?

If 40 Hz feels too buzzy, try a beta beat around 18–20 Hz. It's slower and smoother and is also popular for active concentration. Some people switch between the two depending on the task. See binaural beats for focus for a side-by-side comparison.

Who should be careful

40 Hz binaural beats are just sound, and they're safe for most people at a comfortable volume. If you have epilepsy, a seizure disorder or another neurological condition, talk to a doctor before using any rhythmic audio. Don't listen while driving.

Common questions

How long should I listen to 40 Hz binaural beats?

Match it to your work session: 25 minutes for a Pomodoro, or up to 60 to 90 minutes for a deep-work block. Take a short break between sessions.

Is 40 Hz the same as 40 Hz light or sound therapy?

No. The 40 Hz stimulation studied for brain health uses flickering light or clicking sounds, often both at once. A 40 Hz binaural beat is a smooth pair of tones, one in each ear. They share a frequency, not a method.

Can I use 40 Hz binaural beats for studying?

Yes. It's a popular choice for long study sessions. See the studying guide for which frequencies suit reading, practice problems and exam nerves.
